---
title: "Broomfield Library, and Everything Around It"
date: 2026-08-06
---

Went to the Broomfield library yesterday, August 6th, to get a library card for Libby, and to see what else they had going on.

On the walk in, the flower beds were buzzing with bees and other pollinators working every bloom.

![Ligated Furrow Bee lifting off a black-eyed Susan flower](https://jaredlincenberg.github.io/photos/2026-08-06-broomfield-bee.jpeg)
_A Ligated Furrow Bee (Halictus ligatus) flying off to pollinate another flower. ([observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/388906361))_

Getting the card itself was easy — I'm a Colorado resident, so no trouble there. Getting into the building was a different story. There's a staircase to the left as you walk in, and I was sure it led to a second floor of the library. It didn't! The staff let me know it actually leads to the Broomfield Amphitheater, and we had a good laugh over my honest mistake. They were great: while telling me about their programming, someone pointed me toward the [Broomfield Open Space Action Network](https://broomfieldopenspacefoundation.org/broomfield-open-space-action-network-bosan/), part of the [Broomfield Open Space Foundation](https://broomfieldopenspacefoundation.org/), which I already knew from the [Native Flora and Fauna Broomfield BioBlitz 2026](https://www.inaturalist.org/projects/native-flora-and-fauna-broomfield-bioblitz-2026) project on iNaturalist.

On the way out I had a little time, so I did a lap of the pond at Broomfield Community Park. I heard the unmistakable cry of a Killdeer (*Charadrius vociferus*) before I saw it, then spotted one, and ended up seeing three at once! ([observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/388906745)) Dragonflies and damselflies were everywhere too, though much harder to photograph; [this one](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/388911338) was my best effort. Not the focus of the day, so I didn't let it get to me.

Back near my car, one more pass by the flower bed turned up something small: a Red Sunflower Seed Weevil (*Smicronyx fulvus*), about 3mm, mating ([observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/388907323)).

It was a privilege to go to the Broomfield library and explore the grounds with iNaturalist. In a short time I found 19 native species and 32 in total. I hope to return soon.
