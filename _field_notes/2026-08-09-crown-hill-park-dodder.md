To me, there are few things more exciting than discovering something I just learned about. In mid-2024, I watched a video about a plant genus called Dodder (*Cuscuta*) — [video](https://youtu.be/erIgEEZ_tmo?si=1gnTBhwNMDrqM2lj). It's parasitic, achlorophyllous (without chlorophyll), and orange. It seemed like such a novel and exotic thing, something I wouldn't find in Colorado.

On October 3, 2024, I was doing a lap around Crown Hill Park Lake — a regular walk, since it's close to my work and biologically diverse. Going clockwise from the parking lot, I was exploring the shore where the water had retreated. It was dense with Water Smartweed ([*Persicaria amphibia*](https://www.inaturalist.org/taxa/78450)), interspersed with some other plants that could handle that environment. To my surprise, I saw what looked like silly string covering a bunch of Water Smartweed plants. I was confused and befuddled — who would spray silly string here, on the water's edge, in nature? As I got closer, I saw it was wrapping around the plant, its vines stretching out to the next one. It was then that I thought back to the YouTube video I had watched, noticed the similarities, and realized it was the Dodder the video described. I was in awe of finding a rare and unusual plant — connecting the video's neat, abstract information to something real and right in front of me.

_Cuscuta draped over Water Smartweed at Crown Hill Park Lake — my first sighting, October 3, 2024._

I got plenty of pictures for the iNaturalist observation I was going to make. I made note of the [haustoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haustorium) — little bumps on the vines where it gripped the Smartweed — and saw the flowers, some still in bud and others starting to bloom. [iNaturalist observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/245490499)

{% include photo-embed.html filename="2024-10-03-crown-hill-park-dodder-haustoria.jpeg" alt="Close-up of small bump-like haustoria where an orange Dodder vine wraps around a Water Smartweed stem." %}
_A closer look at the haustoria gripping the Smartweed's stem._

I was so excited that I revisited the plant on October 17, 2024, and it had expanded, covering more *P. amphibia*, with the flowers now blooming. It was a treat to see it again. It was hard to find the exact location — I had the GPS coordinates and remembered roughly where it was, but I still had to search the area to glimpse this devious plant. [iNaturalist observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/247840460)

{% include photo-embed.html filename="2024-10-17-crown-hill-park-dodder-blooming.jpeg" alt="Dodder vines in bloom, tiny white flower clusters among the tangled orange strands over Water Smartweed." %}
_Two weeks later, the Dodder had spread further and come into bloom._

I returned to Crown Hill Park Lake multiple times in 2025 but didn't find any Dodder. It wasn't until August 9, 2026, that I saw the plant again. I found it in a different location on the lake shore, still growing on *P. amphibia* and still looking like it had been sprayed with silly string. Again, I thought back to my previous observations and the video, adding one more link to the story with this plant. [iNaturalist observation](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/389884955)

{% include photo-embed.html filename="2026-08-09-crown-hill-park-dodder-rediscovery.jpeg" alt="Orange Dodder vines wrapped around Water Smartweed in a different spot along the Crown Hill Park Lake shore." %}
_Back again in a new spot along the lake shore, nearly two years later._

I am lucky to have discovered this amazing plant and connected my learning to the real world. So I am now crafting a log of discovery and rediscovery, learning how and why this plant is here. I am excited to think about when I will see this again, or if I can find it elsewhere — another pond in Colorado, or a different area. Every walk, I could be surprised by this cunning plant, adding to our tale and my exploration.
